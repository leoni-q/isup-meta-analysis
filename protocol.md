## 1. Introduction

The aim of this systematic review is to evaluate how the detection of ISUP (International Society of Urological Pathology) grade group 1 prostate cancer has evolved in regard to different prostate cancer screening strategies. By synthesizing evidence from population-based screening studies across distinct diagnostic eras, we seek to describe temporal trends in low-grade cancer detection and to contextualize these findings within the ongoing shift toward more selective, risk-adapted screening approaches. This study evaluates the impact of evolving screening strategies on the detection of low-grade prostate cancer, providing insight into overdiagnosis in the era of renewed interest in population-based screening.

## 2. PICOS Framework

### Patients

Inclusion criteria:

- General population of asymptomatic men undergoing systematized screening for prostate cancer.
- Patients undergoing prostate biopsy according to predefined screening algorithms within randomized controlled trials.

Exclusion criteria:

- Patients previously diagnosed with or treated for prostate cancer.

### Intervention

Inclusion criteria:

- Prostate cancer screening strategies, including for example no testing, PSA-based screening, PSA combined with digital rectal examination, PSA followed by MRI, MRI as a primary screening tool, biomarker-based strategies, and combined MRI and biomarker approaches.
- Different methods of prostate cancer biopsy and/or diagnostic work-up.

Exclusion criteria:

- Work-up diagnostic in patients considered for prostate cancer treatment.

### Comparator

- Different screening strategies.
- Different biopsy strategies.

### Outcome

Primary outcome:

- Detection rate of ISUP grade 1 clinically insignificant cancers.

Secondary outcomes:

- Detection rates of clinically significant prostate cancer (ISUP grade group ≥2).
- Number of prostate biopsies performed.
- Percentage of patients receiving curative treatment over the course of follow-up.

### Study

Inclusion criteria:

- Randomized controlled clinical trials.
- Secondary analyses of large randomized controlled trials, such as ERSPC, CAP, PLCO, and Göteborg trials, that include additional diagnostic analyses relevant to screening strategy, diagnostic work-up, biopsy strategy, or ISUP grade group detection.

## 3. Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria

We will include studies according to the criteria defined using the PICOS framework. In addition to studies with primary or secondary outcomes directly aligned with the objectives of this review, we will also include relevant randomized controlled trials in which the primary or secondary outcomes differ (e.g., prostate cancer-specific or all-cause mortality), provided that these studies report extractable data on prostate cancer detection stratified by ISUP grade group. Specifically, trials reporting the detection of ISUP grade group 1 and/or clinically significant prostate cancer (ISUP grade group ≥2), either as predefined outcomes or as part of baseline or screening-phase analyses, will be considered eligible. Such studies will be included when sufficient data on prostate cancer detection are available to allow meaningful extraction and analysis, even if cancer detection was not the primary focus of the trial.

Eligible studies may include secondary analyses of large randomized controlled screening trials, including but not limited to ERSPC, CAP, PLCO, and Göteborg, where these reports address additional diagnostic analyses, screening algorithms, biopsy strategies, diagnostic work-up, or cancer detection stratified by ISUP grade group or Gleason score. Such secondary analyses will be considered eligible when they derive from randomized screening trial populations and provide extractable data relevant to the review outcomes.

Records will be screened independently by two authors at abstract screening. Included reports will undergo independent screening by two co-authors at full-text screening, and reason for exclusion will be provided. We will include only randomized controlled trials and eligible secondary analyses derived from randomized controlled screening trials. Studies reported exclusively as conference abstracts without a full peer-reviewed publication will be excluded due to insufficient methodological and outcome data. Studies conducted in symptomatic populations or in clinical diagnostic settings outside organized or population-based screening programs will be excluded. In particular, studies in which prostate biopsy is performed solely within a diagnostic setting (i.e., triggered by clinical suspicion rather than a predefined screening protocol) will not be considered. Only studies in which the initial population is defined at the level of screening (i.e., asymptomatic men undergoing systematic or randomized screening) will be eligible for inclusion.

Non-English language manuscripts will be excluded during the screening.

Editorial comments and letters to editors will be screened for insights relevant to the interpretation of data.

## 4. Search Strategy

The following databases will be searched for records of interest: MEDLINE (via PubMed), Embase, Web of Science Core Collection, and top 200 records from the Google Scholar search engine. No time or language filters will be imposed. In case of duplicates, most recent records reporting relevant data will be included. The initial records will be retrieved from databases, combined, and deduplicated using the Rayyan.ai software (Ouzzani et al., 2016). The title and abstract screening will be performed independently by two authors using predefined eligibility criteria. The qualified records will then be subject to full-text screening by two independent authors. Following abstract screening, selected reports will be retrieved, and undergo full-text screening by two independent authors. The reports selected for the review will undergo backwards citation searching to identify additional records of interest. At each point of the search strategy, any disagreements between reviewers will be settled through consensus with co-authors.

## 5. Data Extraction

We will include studies reporting on screening strategies in prostate cancer described in the literature. Non-relevant reports will be excluded with a reason.

Extracted variables will include study characteristics (author, year, country, study design), population characteristics (sample size, age range), screening strategy, type of biopsy, number of biopsies performed, number and proportion of detected prostate cancers, and histopathological outcomes including ISUP grade group 1 and ISUP grade group ≥2 disease. Data on intervention-related toxicity will also be collected to better interpret the benefits of each screening strategy. In particular, data on adverse events related to screening and diagnostic procedures will be extracted where available, including complications associated with prostate biopsy (e.g., infections, sepsis, bleeding, urinary retention) and information on biopsy approach (transrectal vs transperineal) and use of antibiotic prophylaxis. The extracted data will be entered to Microsoft Excel spreadsheets by two co-authors independently, and then merged, and conflicts will be solved through mediation with co-authors.

## 6. Endpoint Definitions

Primary endpoint:

- Detection rate and proportion of ISUP grade group 1 prostate cancer among screened men or among detected prostate cancers.

Secondary endpoints:

- Detection rate of clinically significant prostate cancer (defined as ISUP grade group ≥2).
- Number of prostate biopsies performed per screening strategy.

When ISUP grading was not explicitly reported, Gleason score ≤6 disease will be considered equivalent to ISUP grade group 1.

## 7. Risk of Bias Assessment

The risk of bias will be assessed independently by two reviewers. Randomized controlled trials will be evaluated using the Cochrane Risk of Bias 2 (RoB 2) tool. Any disagreements will be resolved through discussion or adjudication by a third reviewer. Individual analyses of risk of bias for each study will be published in a Supplementary File, and reasons for assessing studies at high risk of bias will be discussed in the manuscript.

## 8. Statistical Analysis

The study will be reported according to the PRISMA 2020 statement (Page et al., 2021), and will follow guidelines described in Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions.

First, data on included studies will be tabularized and described systematically. Studies will be grouped according to the screening strategy applied, including digital rectal examination (DRE), PSA-based screening, PSA combined with DRE, PSA followed by MRI, MRI-based screening strategies, and other combined or biomarker-based approaches.

For the purposes of meta-analysis, rates of ISUP grade group 1 and ISUP grade group ≥2 prostate cancer detection will be pooled from eligible randomized controlled trial as risk ratios for comparative outcomes, and as proportions for each arm separately, stratified by the screening strategy.

Log-transformed risk ratios and proportions will be pooled in random-effects meta-analysis models due to expected clinical heterogeneity. For risk ratios, inverse-variance DerSimonian-Laird models will be used to estimate Tau, with Hartung-Knapp adjustment for confidence interval calculation. For proportions, generalized linear mixed models will be used. For both analyses, 0.5 continuity adjustment will be added to cells with zero frequency.

Heterogeneity will be assessed using Cochran's Q test (p<0.05 considered significant). In case of evidence for significant heterogeneity, explorative qualitative and quantitative analyses will be performed. Leave-one-out sensitivity analyses will be used to assess robustness. Publication bias will be assessed using Peter's linear regression and funnel plots in analyses containing 10 or more studies.

Outcomes will be presented on forest plots, including back-transformed outcome measures with corresponding confidence intervals.

## 9. Data Synthesis

The study methodology will follow the PRISMA 2020 statement guidelines (Page et al., 2021). As a part of the systematic review, the results of included studies will be tabularised and presented in a standardised way, including basic clinical characteristics of included patients. Studies will be grouped according to the screening strategy applied, including digital rectal examination (DRE), PSA-based screening, PSA combined with DRE, PSA followed by MRI, MRI-based screening strategies, and other combined or biomarker-based approaches.

For the purposes of meta-analysis, data on ISUP grade group 1 and ISUP grade group ≥2 prostate cancer detection will be extracted from each eligible randomized controlled trial. The proportion of ISUP grade group 1 and ISUP grade group ≥2 cancers will be presented in relation to the total number of men undergoing screening within each study arm.

Specifically, when studies report the number of detected prostate cancers stratified by ISUP grade group or Gleason score categories, the following measures will be calculated:

- Detection rates per number of screened men, where denominators are available.
- The proportion of ISUP grade group 1 cancers among all detected prostate cancers within each study arm.
- The proportion of clinically significant cancers (ISUP grade group ≥2) among all detected prostate cancers within each study arm.

When ISUP grading is not explicitly reported, Gleason score 6 will be considered equivalent to ISUP grade group 1, and Gleason score ≥7 will be considered equivalent to ISUP grade group ≥2. Where necessary, numerator and denominator data will be extracted from tables, supplementary materials, or appendices to reconstruct the relevant outcome measures. Additionally, the ratio of the total number of prostate biopsies performed to the number of men undergoing screening will be calculated for each study arm, where data are available, and the percentage of patients receiving curative treatment over the course of follow-up. A conceptual figure illustrating the evolution of screening strategies and their impact on low-grade prostate cancer detection will be included.

Where reported, adverse events and procedural harms associated with different screening and diagnostic strategies will be summarized using Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE). These data will not be treated as formal endpoints but will be comparatively assessed across studies to provide contextual interpretation of screening strategies and their clinical burden.
